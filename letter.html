Editor's Letter

Welcome to the Underclass

Why this magazine exists, and who it is for.

Jacob Garcia - Editor-in-Chief - Chicago

Every magazine about technology eventually becomes a magazine about the industry: the funding, the founders, the keynote, the roadmap. Those magazines are fine. This is not one of them.

Underclass starts from a different observation. The most important story in technology right now is not what the machines can do. It is what is happening to people while the machines learn to do it. The graduate whose first rung got automated. The worker in Nairobi teaching a model to see. The man who loves a voice that lives in an app. The kid on a bike riding past a building full of other people's thoughts. That is the beat. People, and their relationships to things that are accelerating faster than any relationship can.

The name is a claim. A new class structure is forming, and it is not sorted by money, not at first. It is sorted by position relative to the machine: above the algorithm, inside it, or underneath it. The executives and researchers are above it. Most of us are inside it, scored, ranked, recommended, routed. And underneath it, always, is someone doing the part that still requires a body, a box-drawing hand, a human witness. That someone is the underclass, and if the current trajectory holds, it is all of us, on a long enough timeline.

None of this makes us pessimists. Acceleration is real, and a lot of it is good: the drug discovery, the cheap energy, the tools landing in the hands of kids who could never have afforded them. We intend to cover that too. But the cheerleading is well funded already. What is underfunded is the witness: careful, human-scale attention paid to the people standing where the future lands first.

That is the job. Issue one is in front of you. We hope it reads like what it is: a magazine made fast, in Chicago, by people who believe the speed of everything deserves better writing about it.

- J.G.
