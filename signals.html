Issue 01 / Fall 2026

Signals

Speed readings from the acceleration. Short, sharp, and true as of press time.

Robots

The humanoids clocked in

Humanoid robots have moved from demo videos to paying shifts in warehouses and factories, still clumsy, still supervised, and no longer funny. The question stopped being "can they work" and became "who owns the shift they work."

Agents

Errands are an API now

AI agents quietly took over the low-grade admin of middle-class life: booking, rescheduling, disputing, unsubscribing. Nobody throws a parade for a chore, but add up the hours and the machines have already given people back a day a month. Notice who can afford the agents.

Energy

The grid met the growth curve

Data center demand has turned electricity planning into a contact sport. Rates, substations, and nuclear restarts are now AI policy, whether the voters connecting those dots or not. The cloud has a meter, and this year the meter started running loud enough to hear.

Music

The charts can't tell, and stopped asking

Synthetic artists now chart beside human ones, and listeners mostly shrug. The controversy migrated from "is it real music" to "who owns the voiceprint," which is a lawyers' way of admitting the audience left years ago.

Work

The office came back, sort of, watching

Return-to-office mandates met AI productivity scoring, and the hybrid result is the weirdest workplace in memory: half the staff in a building, all of the staff on a dashboard. Presence used to be a fact. It is now a metric.
